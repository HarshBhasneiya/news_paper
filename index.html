VOLCANIC ERUPTION

A volcanic eruption is a powerful geological event that occurs when magma (molten rock), volcanic ash, debris, and hot gases are forcefully expelled from the Earth's interior through a vent or fracture in the crust.
Driven by tremendous heat and pressure beneath the surface, eruptions act as a planetary cooling mechanism.
They vary drastically in intensity, ranging from gentle, slow-moving rivers of lava to cataclysmic, violent explosions that can shoot ash plumes miles into the atmosphere.
🌋 The Underlying MechanismThe process behind an eruption can be broken down into three fundamental stages:Magma Formation: Deep within the Earth's mantle, extreme heat and pressure partially melt solid rock, transforming it into a thick fluid called magma.
The Ascent: Because magma is less dense and lighter than the solid rock surrounding it, it naturally migrates upward. It aggregates close to the surface inside massive chambers known as magma pockets or chambers.The Breach: As more magma fills the chamber, dissolved gases expand and create intense upward pressure. When this internal magmatic pressure or gas buoyancy overcomes the downward weight of the structural crust above it, the crust fractures.
The magma violently or steadily rushes through these conduits to exit the surface vent.
Once magma breaks through to the surface, it is officially classified as lava.
